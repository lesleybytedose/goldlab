Reading this app

R — the only unit that matters

One R is whatever you risked. If your stop sits $4 from entry, that trade risks 1R. A target twice as far away pays 2R.

Using R instead of rands lets a $10 trade and a $1,000 trade be compared honestly, and stops a few big wins hiding a lot of small losses.

Expectancy

The average R across every finished trade. +0.20R means each trade made a fifth of what it risked, on average. −0.10R means each one quietly cost you a tenth.

Win rate alone tells you nothing — you can win 80% of the time and still go broke if the losses are big enough.

The range after the number

An expectancy of +0.17R is an estimate, not a fact. The app shows the range the true value plausibly sits in. If that range crosses zero, the model has not been shown to work — however good the headline looks.

Drawdown and losing runs

A model averaging +0.2R that once lost fourteen trades in a row is unusable on a small account, because you would be wiped out before the average arrived.

Worst drawdown is the deepest fall from a high point. Look at it before the expectancy, not after.

Why there is a random control

One model flips a coin, using the same stops and targets as the rest. It shows what risk structure alone produces, with no skill involved. A model that cannot beat it has found nothing.

Where the numbers come from

Every signal was written to disk the moment its candle closed, before the outcome existed. Results fill in later when price reaches stop or target.

Nothing here is estimated, adjusted, or typed in by hand.
